# Global-road-accident-analysis

# Project Overview

Road accidents remain a significant public safety concern, resulting in injuries, fatalities, property damage, and economic losses. Understanding the patterns behind these incidents is essential for identifying high-risk groups, improving road safety policies, and developing effective accident prevention strategies.

For this project, I developed an interactive **Road Accident Analysis Dashboard using Microsoft Power BI** to explore accident occurrences, casualty figures, severity levels, and the factors associated with road incidents.

The dashboard transforms raw accident data into meaningful visual insights, enabling users to examine accident trends across countries, years, months, time of day, weather conditions, driver age groups, gender, and contributing factors.

# Project Objectives

The primary objectives of this project were to:

- Analyze the distribution of road accidents and casualties.
- Identify countries with the highest recorded accident occurrences and fatalities.
- Determine which age groups account for the most incidents and fatalities.
- Examine accident severity, including property damage, minor injuries, serious injuries, and fatal cases.
- Investigate the relationship between speeding and accident severity.
- Identify major contributing factors, including speeding, driving under the influence, distraction, fatigue, weather, and road defects.
- Explore accident patterns by time of day, month, year, and weather condition.
- Develop data-driven recommendations to support road safety planning and accident prevention.

# Tools and Techniques

**Tool:** Microsoft Power BI

The project involved data preparation, data visualization, KPI development, and interactive dashboard design. Charts and tables were used to present accident distributions, compare categories, and highlight patterns that may require further investigation.

The dashboard includes KPI cards for accident cases and casualties, horizontal bar charts for country comparisons and contributing factors, column charts for age groups and accident severity, and additional visuals for weather, time of day, gender, and safety measures.

# Business Value

The dashboard provides a consolidated view of road accident data, helping road safety agencies, policymakers, transport organizations, and other stakeholders identify priority areas for investigation.

By highlighting accident patterns and potential risk factors, the analysis can support more targeted road safety campaigns, enforcement strategies, infrastructure improvements, and driver education initiatives.


## Key insights from the dashboard

# Accident cases
Insight 1: Road accidents are substantial in scale
The dashboard reports approximately 1,048,575 accident cases and 1.58 million casualties. This indicates a considerable recorded burden of road incidents within the dataset.

Implication: Road safety interventions should address both accident prevention and the reduction of injuries and fatalities.

# Highest displayed
Insight 2: The USA leads the displayed country comparisons
The USA records approximately 16,502 fatalities in the top-seven fatality chart and 156,799 accident occurrences in the country comparison. China and India follow in both displayed rankings.
Implication: These countries may warrant closer examination of their accident patterns. However, absolute totals should be compared alongside population, vehicle ownership, traffic volume, and distance travelled before drawing conclusions about relative road safety.

# Adult drivers
Insight 3: Adult drivers account for the most recorded incidents
Adult drivers account for approximately 514,724 incidents, compared with 226,894 among young adult drivers, 221,746 among elderly drivers, and 85,211 among underage drivers.
Adult drivers also have the highest displayed fatality count, at 33,900.
Implication: Adult drivers represent an important group for road safety education and enforcement. These figures describe counts, however, and do not establish that adults have the highest individual risk without exposure data.


# Property damage
Insight 4: Property damage and minor injuries dominate the severity categories
Property-damage-only cases account for approximately 231,259 incidents, while minor injuries account for 231,163. Serious injuries total 145,170, and fatal cases total 52,574.
Implication: Prevention strategies should address both frequent lower-severity incidents and the smaller but more serious group of fatal and severe-injury incidents.

# Speeding
Insight 5: Speeding is the leading displayed contributing factor
Speeding records approximately 209,792 cases, followed by driving under the influence (188,994), distraction (157,163), fatigue (125,156), and weather (105,663).
The speeding severity chart also displays 605,117 cases associated with overspeeding and 443,458 within the speed limit.
Implication: Speed management is a priority area for further investigation. The severity chart should be validated to confirm exactly what the values represent and whether they count accidents, casualties, or another measure.


# Clear conditions
Insight 6: Most recorded incidents occur in clear weather conditions
Clear weather accounts for approximately 628,005 incidents, compared with 209,381 in rain, 105,076 in snow, 53,114 at night, and 52,999 in fog.
Implication: Road safety programmes should not focus exclusively on adverse weather. Clear conditions may account for more incidents because of greater driving exposure, traffic volume, or other factors. Weather-specific accident rates are needed to establish comparative risk.


# Daytime
Insight 7: Daytime accounts for the highest accident count
Approximately 713,953 incidents occur during the day, compared with 230,264 at night and 104,358 during dawn or dusk.
Implication: Daytime road safety deserves significant attention, including congestion management, speed compliance, driver awareness, and pedestrian safety. The number of incidents alone does not establish that daytime driving is more dangerous per trip.


# Male drivers
Insight 8: Male drivers account for more recorded incidents
Male drivers account for approximately 733,740 incidents, compared with 293,953 involving female drivers and 20,882 where gender is unknown.
Implication: Further analysis could examine driver exposure, age, driving frequency, vehicle type, and incident severity. These counts alone do not demonstrate that male drivers have a higher accident rate.


# Seatbelt used
Insight 9: Most recorded cases involve seatbelt use
The dashboard reports approximately 918,659 cases with seatbelts used and 129,916 without seatbelt use. Airbags were deployed in 569,363 recorded cases.
Implication: The high number of cases involving seatbelt use reinforces the need to distinguish between accident occurrence and injury outcomes. Seatbelts may reduce injury severity without necessarily preventing an accident. A severity comparison between belted and unbelted occupants would be more informative.


# Recommendations
1. Strengthen speed management
Prioritize speed-limit enforcement, speed-camera deployment where appropriate, road design that encourages safe speeds, and public education about the consequences of speeding. Use the data to identify locations and periods where speeding-related incidents are concentrated.

2. Reduce impaired and distracted driving
Expand public awareness campaigns, enforce laws against driving under the influence, discourage mobile-phone use while driving, and promote responsible driving practices. Monitor changes in incident frequency and severity to assess the effectiveness of these measures.

3. Target road safety education at relevant driver groups
Develop targeted programmes for adult drivers, young adults, and other groups identified through the analysis. Include defensive driving, hazard awareness, fatigue management, and safe driving practices. Avoid treating raw accident counts as evidence of individual risk without considering exposure.

4. Improve weather and road-condition preparedness
Maintain road surfaces and drainage, improve visibility through road markings and lighting, and communicate weather-related hazards. Compare accident rates under different weather conditions rather than relying only on total counts.

5. Promote seatbelt and vehicle-safety compliance
Continue seatbelt enforcement and public education, while assessing the relationship between seatbelt use, airbag deployment, and injury severity. Use these findings to support occupant-protection policies.

6. Prioritize high-burden locations using normalized measures
Investigate countries or regions with high accident and fatality counts. Where possible, calculate fatalities per 100,000 people, per registered vehicle, or per distance travelled to distinguish large traffic volumes from elevated road safety risk.

7. Monitor accident trends and evaluate interventions
Track monthly and annual accident counts, fatality rates, injury severity, and contributing factors. Investigate unusual increases, validate data quality, and compare performance before and after road safety interventions.


# Further analysis to strengthen the project
To make this project even more valuable as a data analytics portfolio piece, consider adding the following:
- Fatality rate: Fatal accidents divided by total accidents, expressed as a percentage.
- Severity rate: Serious injuries and fatalities as a proportion of all recorded cases.
- Year-over-year change: Measure whether accident counts and fatalities are increasing or decreasing.
- Monthly trend analysis: Identify seasonal patterns and peak accident periods.
- Speeding severity comparison: Compare fatality and serious-injury rates for speeding versus non-speeding incidents.
- Seatbelt effectiveness analysis: Compare the distribution of injury severity among belted and unbelted occupants.
- Country-level normalized rates: Add population or vehicle-exposure data to make country comparisons more meaningful.
One important data-quality note: the dashboard's reported accident total, casualty total, severity categories, and some country-level charts appear to use different counts or possibly different measures. Before publishing the figures as definitive conclusions, verify the data definitions, aggregation methods, and whether each visual counts accidents, casualties, fatalities, or contributing-factor records.

# Conclusion
This Road Accident Analysis Dashboard demonstrates how Microsoft Power BI can be used to explore complex accident data and present it in a form that supports decision-making.
The findings highlight several priority areas for road safety investigation, including speeding, impaired driving, distraction, driver demographics, weather conditions, and occupant protection.

The most important takeaway is that effective road safety planning requires more than counting accidents; it requires understanding where, when, and under what conditions severe incidents occur.

By combining descriptive analysis with more detailed risk calculations, stakeholders can develop better-targeted interventions, allocate resources more effectively, and monitor progress toward safer roads.

This project strengthened my skills in data analysis, dashboard design, data storytelling, and translating visual findings into practical recommendations.

The ultimate goal is to demonstrate how data analytics can help transform accident records into practical insights that inform road safety decisions.
